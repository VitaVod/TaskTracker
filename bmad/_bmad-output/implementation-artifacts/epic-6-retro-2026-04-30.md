# Epic 6 Retrospective: Trust, Moderation, and Support Operations

Date: 2026-04-30
Epic: Epic 6
Status: Completed Retrospective
Stories: 5 planned, 5 marked done in sprint status

## Scope Reviewed

- Story 6.1: Build admin suspicious-activity review workspace
- Story 6.2: Implement moderation actions with safety guards
- Story 6.3: Build support diagnostic view for user progress disputes
- Story 6.4: Implement event timeline and correlation-based troubleshooting
- Story 6.5: Implement immutable audit logging for privileged actions

## Executive Summary

Epic 6 delivered the internal trust and operations capability set end-to-end: admin users can review suspicious activity and apply guarded moderation actions, support users can inspect read-only diagnostic state and timeline evidence, and privileged operations now flow through an immutable, queryable audit model with correlation and trace continuity.

The strongest outcome is consistent role-boundary and explainability behavior across the entire operations surface. Admin and support capabilities follow policy-restricted endpoints, standardized Problem Details error contracts, deterministic ordering, and read-only constraints where required. Correlation-aware telemetry and audit entries improve dispute resolution and accountability.

The primary concern is closure hygiene and operational maturity. Story metadata and sprint-level status were briefly inconsistent, and while observability counters/logs are present, explicit runbooks and alert playbooks for internal operations workflows remain thinner than code-level implementation detail.

## What Went Well

1. Admin suspicious-case review was delivered as a safe read-first operations surface
- Story 6.1 introduced deterministic suspicious-case listing, admin-only access control, bounded querying, and UI states tailored to operator workflows.

2. Moderation actions implemented with explicit safety guardrails
- Story 6.2 added policy-guarded moderation commands, destructive-action confirmation requirements, idempotent intent handling, and immutable moderation-aligned evidence capture.

3. Support diagnostics achieved read-only, high-context dispute triage
- Story 6.3 consolidated account/task/xp/streak diagnostics into a support-only view with role isolation, bounded query contracts, and no mutation controls.

4. Timeline-based explainability significantly improved troubleshooting quality
- Story 6.4 added correlation-aware event timelines with deterministic ordering and filterable event/date windows, enabling clearer root-cause narratives for users and operators.

5. Privileged audit model matured into centralized immutable evidence path
- Story 6.5 introduced generalized privileged-action audit persistence, centralized writer integration, bounded retrieval endpoints, and UI visibility for compliance-oriented review.

## What Did Not Go Well

1. Story-level and sprint-level state drift occurred during closeout
- Story 6.5 artifact header indicates review while sprint status marks the story done, creating avoidable ambiguity for release-readiness checks.

2. Epic-level completion status was not closed automatically
- All Epic 6 stories were done, but the epic remained in-progress until retrospective closure update.

3. Ops-readiness documentation is still less mature than implementation
- Logging, metrics, and audit fields are implemented, but incident response runbooks and threshold definitions are not yet uniformly captured alongside feature artifacts.

## Key Risks Going Into Epic 7

1. Integration feature work may bypass or underuse privileged audit patterns
- Epic 7 introduces external pathways; if integration failures or privileged support actions are not consistently audited, troubleshooting quality and compliance evidence may regress.

2. Correlation continuity risk across first-party and integration event sources
- Timeline and audit views now rely on stable correlation metadata; inconsistent correlation propagation in new integration flows would reduce explainability.

3. Operational load growth may expose missing internal playbooks
- Internal tools now exist and may be used more frequently as integration complexity grows; absent escalation guidance and alert thresholds could increase support latency.

## Lessons Learned

1. Read-first operations surfaces reduce risk before introducing mutation controls
- Sequencing admin review (6.1) before moderation commands (6.2) lowered accidental-action risk and made policy requirements explicit.

2. Correlation-aware contracts improve both support speed and trust narratives
- Timeline and audit consistency across stories made dispute explanations more deterministic and less dependent on ad hoc log spelunking.

3. Centralized privileged auditing is more sustainable than per-feature audit variants
- Moving toward generalized privileged-action capture in Story 6.5 reduces duplication and simplifies compliance validation.
